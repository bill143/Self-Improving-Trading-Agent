# Phase 7 — Sales Readiness Playbook
**Agent:** sales-closer-agent · **Phase:** 7 (final foundation phase)
**Niche (locked Phase 1):** Residential home services — HVAC, plumbing & electrical contractors, US, owner-operated, 5–50 technicians (roofing/solar adjacent)
**Buyer:** the owner. Not the office manager, not the marketing coordinator. If the owner is not on the call, we reschedule — a 5–50 tech shop owner signs his own checks.
**What we sell on call one:** Pillars 1+2 starter bundle — AI database reactivation + reviews & referrals system.
**Status:** BUILT + drill-tested (see §11). Ready for the first real BOOKED appointment.

---

## 0. Pre-call prep — 10 minutes, non-negotiable

Never dial without these five facts. Discovery is for numbers only he has; anything public is homework.

| Pull before the call | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Google review count + star rating | Google Business Profile | Opens Pillar 2 with a fact, not a question |
| Date of most recent review | GBP | "Your last review is from March" is a scalpel |
| Trade mix + franchise brand (if any) | Website / Phase 2 target list | Franchise = discount rules apply; reactivation offer is the wedge |
| Tech count / truck count | Website "our team", fleet photos | Sizes the database and the capacity cap |
| Does the site have a form, and does it have an emergency checkbox | Site audit | Pre-loads the Pillar 3 upsell for day 30 |

**Show-rate protocol (KPI #1 — a close rate on a call that never happens is zero):**
- Confirmation SMS within 60 seconds of booking.
- 24h before: "Randy — still good for our 2pm tomorrow? Reply Y or tell me a better time. — Bill"
- 1h before: "Calling you at 2 on the number I've got. Anything you want me to look at before we talk?"
- No-show → call at +5 min, SMS at +10 min ("looks like we missed each other — want to grab another time?"), then one rebook attempt at 48h. Two misses and the lead goes back to CONTACTED, not DNC.

**Language discipline — banned words, every call:**
| Never say | Always say |
|---|---|
| price, cost, fee, charge | **investment** |
| contract | agreement |
| sign up | get started |
| package | program |
| I think / probably / hopefully | (delete — say the number or don't) |

## 1. STEP 1 — INTRO (1–2 min, then take the wheel)

**The unfair advantage line.** Bill runs O'Neill Contractors. This niche's default posture toward marketers is contempt. We collapse it in the first fifteen seconds by not being one.

> "Randy? Bill. Appreciate you carving out the time — I know 2pm on a Tuesday in [month] isn't nothing.
> Quick bit of context so you know who you're talking to: I come out of contracting, not marketing. I run O'Neill Contractors. So I'm not going to talk to you about impressions and engagement — I don't care about those and neither do you.
> Before I take up your afternoon: how's your week actually going? You buried, or is it slow?"

Two beats of human. Then take control — explicitly:

> "Here's how I run these, so there's no mystery. I'm going to ask you a bunch of nosy questions about your numbers for about fifteen minutes — spend, leads, booking rate, what you actually keep. If what I do can't help you, I'll tell you that and give you your time back. If it can, I'll walk you through exactly how it works and you can tell me yes or no today. Fair?"

**Why the "yes or no today" is planted here:** it pre-frames the close so it isn't ambush at minute 40. A contractor who agrees to decide will decide.

**Rules:** stand up, talk slower than he does, never apologize for the meeting, never say "I just wanted to reach out." If he opens with "so what is this, marketing?" — answer flatly: "Kind of. I fix two specific holes that cost you jobs. I'll show you which ones and you tell me if they're real."

## 2. STEP 2 — DISCOVERY (15–20 min, black-and-white numbers)

Write every number down and **say it back to him**. The doubt is built by the arithmetic, not by adjectives. If he doesn't know a number, that is itself the finding: *"You're running a $4M business and nobody in the building knows that number. That's not a criticism — that's the gap."*

### 2.1 The scorecard — collect all of these

| # | Question (ask verbatim) | Number captured | What it opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What are you spending on marketing a month, all in — LSA, Google, the agency, truck wraps, all of it?" | $/mo | Baseline; the compare-to number at close |
| 2 | "How many leads does that buy you a month — calls plus web forms?" | leads/mo | Denominator for everything |
| 3 | "Of those, how many actually get on the schedule?" | booking % | Usually guessed high by 15 pts |
| 4 | "Where does that number come from — the dashboard, or your gut?" | (source) | Builds doubt without insulting him |
| 5 | "How many of those booked actually let the tech in the door?" | show % | Pillar 3 reminder value |
| 6 | "Of the replacement quotes you write, what percent close?" | close % | The multiplier in every later calc |
| 7 | "What's your average repair ticket? And a full changeout?" | $ / $ | The ROI math. HVAC changeout typically $5k–$12.5k |
| 8 | "What'd you do in revenue last year, and what did you actually keep after everything?" | $ / net % | Emotional pain lives here |
| 9 | "How many records are in ServiceTitan/Housecall/Jobber total?" | records | Pillar 1 raw material |
| 10 | "Of those, how many called or filled out a form 6+ months ago and never got invoiced?" | dormant | **The asset he's sitting on** |
| 11 | "How many appointments a week could you absorb if I handed them to you tomorrow?" | capacity | Gate 2 of Pillar 1; also proves we won't drown him |
| 12 | "How many calls hit the phone and nobody picks up? What happens at 6pm and on Sunday?" | missed/mo | Pillar 5 (voice AI), planted for later |
| 13 | "Somebody fills out your website form at 9 at night — when do they hear back?" | response time | Pillar 3, planted for day 30 |
| 14 | "How do you get reviews right now?" | (method) | Pillar 2 — the answer is always "the techs hand out cards" |
| 15 | "How many did you get last month? What's your rating?" | count / stars | Confront against what I pulled pre-call |
| 16 | "How many jobs last month came from a referral — and how do you know?" | count / (untracked) | Pillar 2 back half |
| 17 | "Where do you want the business in 12 months — number and what it buys you?" | $ goal + the *why* | The "where you want to be" in the close |

### 2.2 The four pillar-doubt questions (ask them consecutively, don't cushion)
1. "How do you get reviews?"
2. "How do you handle missed calls?"
3. "How fast is your lead follow-up — actual minutes, not policy?"
4. "What happens to a lead that says 'not right now'?"

Then sit in the silence. Four unsatisfying answers in a row does the work no pitch can.

### 2.3 Say the arithmetic back to him — out loud, slowly

> "Let me make sure I've got this. 120 leads a month. Call it 45% get booked — so 54. You're paying $6,500 for the whole thing. That means you're buying 66 leads a month that never make it onto a truck. At your changeout number, one of those a month is the whole marketing budget. Am I doing that math wrong?"

Never soften it and never gloat. Contractors respect the number; they resent the tone.

### 2.4 THE KILLER QUESTION (verbatim, then stop talking)

> "Randy — realistically, with your current strategy, do you think you're going to hit [his stated goal] in the next 3 to 6 months if you don't change anything?"

Whatever he says, do not rescue him. Count to three. Then Step 3.

## 3. STEP 3 — TRANSITION (30 seconds)

> "Before I show you anything — is there anything else we haven't covered that would help me better understand your situation?"

This surfaces the hidden obstacle *before* it becomes a fake objection at the close. In this niche the things that come out here are: a partner or spouse on the books, a franchise agreement, an existing agency on a term, a seasonal cash crunch (Feb–March for HVAC), or a bad experience with a marketer. **Whatever he names here, write it down verbatim — it must be resolved in Step 6, and its exact words go in the Step 5 pitch.**

---

## 4. STEP 4 — AUTHORITY POSITIONING (15 seconds, ask permission)

> "Randy, based on what we've covered, I've got a good understanding of where you're at and where you want to get to. My area of expertise is helping HVAC and plumbing owners just like you — 5 to 50 trucks, owner still in the business. Can I walk you through our process?"

Wait for the yes. Do not pitch over a "sure, I guess." If it's lukewarm: "You don't sound sure. What's on your mind?" — better to eat the objection now than at minute 45.

## 5. STEP 5 — PITCH (logical pain → emotional pain → AI solution → benefit, in his words)

Structure per pillar, then **stop and ask**. Never stack both pillars into one monologue.

### 5.1 PILLAR 1 — AI Database Reactivation
**Logical pain:** "You told me you've got about 4,300 people in ServiceTitan who called you, got a number, and never bought. That's not a list — that's people who already raised their hand about a specific broken thing in their house, and your team ran out of hours before they ran out of interest."

**Emotional pain:** "You already paid for every one of those. LSA money, Google money, your CSR's time. That's spent. It's sitting in a database doing nothing while you write another check for new leads next month."

**AI solution (mechanism, plainly):** "We pull the dormant records — inquired six-plus months ago, never invoiced. Scrub them against your DNC, anyone active, anyone with an open estimate. Then a three-day text and email sequence goes out **in your name, from your service manager**, not from an agency. Day one is an apology — 'you called about your AC in June and we never got it sorted.' Day two is the calendar. Day three is a clean deadline and permission to say no. Every reply gets answered in under five minutes, gets offered two specific times, and lands on your board already confirmed with a 24-hour and a 2-hour reminder."

**Their words back:** *"You know how you told me [his phrase]… what we do is [the pillar]… which means [his stated outcome]."*
> "You know how you told me 'we're great once we're in the house, we're just terrible at chasing people'? That's exactly what this is. The chasing is the part that gets automated. Your guys stay in the house."

**The offer that makes it work (and why it's not a discount):** "The hook is a free 21-point health check on the exact system they called about — 30 minutes, written report, honest read. It costs you a truck roll, not margin. That matters because you're a One Hour franchisee and corporate polices discounting — but nobody's agreement says you can't send a service reminder to your own customer list. No corporate approval needed."

**The honest math frame — never a promise:**
> "I'm not going to tell you what you'll get; anybody who does is guessing at your close rate. Here's what I'll tell you. The system is built to a 3 to 6 percent positive-reply benchmark on a scrubbed list, and we throttle it to the 20 appointments a week you said you could absorb. You know your show rate and you know your changeout ticket. Do that math yourself and tell me if it's worth a conversation."

**Then:** "What questions do you have on that specifically?"

### 5.2 PILLAR 2 — Reviews & Referrals
**Logical pain:** "187 reviews, 4.6, and your most recent one is from March. Meanwhile the shop across town is at 400. When a homeowner searches 'AC repair near me,' that's the entire beauty contest — and you told me your techs hand out cards."

**Emotional pain:** "You've got guys doing genuinely good work every day and none of it accumulates anywhere. And the ones who *are* unhappy? You find out when it's already on Google, at midnight, in public, with your name on it."

**AI solution:** "Two to three hours after every job closes in ServiceTitan, the customer gets a text with **your tech's name in the first six words** — 'How'd Dave do, 1 to 5?' Because people don't rate companies, they rate Dave. A 4 or 5 gets a thank-you and *then*, separately, a review link. A 1, 2 or 3 gets a human on the phone within one business hour and **never** gets asked for a review. That's the intercept: the bad ones come to you privately before they go public."

**The referral half:** "Every response — good or bad, identically — enters a quarterly drawing. Every friend they refer who actually books adds five entries. Framed as 'here's how to improve your odds,' not 'do us a favor.' The entry is for the feedback, never for the review — that keeps you compliant with Google, and I won't build it any other way."

**Their words back:**
> "You know how you said 'all my best jobs come from word of mouth but I've got no idea how to make more of it'? This is making more of it, on purpose, from the customers you already served this week."

**The number that matters:** "At 300 completed jobs a month, the model lands around 7 referral jobs a month at zero ad spend. At your changeout ticket, you can price that yourself."

**Then:** "What questions do you have on that specifically?"

### 5.3 What we deliberately do NOT pitch on call one
Speed-to-lead (Pillar 3), paid ads (Pillar 4) and voice AI (Pillar 5) are **named as diagnosed problems in discovery and then parked**:
> "Your 24-hour form response and your 35 missed calls a month are real money and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But I'm not selling you those today. Pillar one pays for itself out of a database you already own — no new ad spend. We'll look at the phone problem at your 30-day review with real numbers on the table."

**Why:** it is credible, it lowers the first-call investment, and it makes the day-30 upsell a scheduled event rather than a cold pitch. Ads are *blocked* until reactivation cash lands (Pillar 1 handoff) — never sell them out of order.

## 6. STEP 6 — TEMPERATURE CHECK & RE-LOOP

> "On a scale of 1 to 10 — 1 being you don't think this would help you at all, 10 being you're ready to start right now — where would you say you fall?"

**9 or 10 →** go straight to Step 7.
**Under 9 → the re-loop, in this exact order:**
1. "Okay — why aren't you a 1?" *(He now sells himself. Let him list everything he likes. Do not interrupt. Write it down.)*
2. "That's a solid list. So what would make you a 10?"
3. Isolate: "Other than [that one thing], is there anything else standing between you and getting started today?"
4. Resolve it. Then re-ask the number.

## 7. STEP 7 — CLOSE

### 7.1 The three-step frame (state it before the number)
> "Randy, here's how this works. First we process payment, second we sign the agreement, third we schedule your onboarding call. Does that make sense?"

### 7.2 The terms — stated plainly, every single time, before any card is touched
> "It's a 12-month program with a 30-day out in the first month. Meaning if after that initial month you don't want to continue, you give us 5 days' notice and we part as friends — no penalty, no chasing you."

**Guardrail: no payment is ever processed before that sentence is said out loud.**

### 7.3 The investment
> "The investment to take you from where you're at right now — 66 leads a month falling on the floor and a database of 4,300 people you already paid for doing nothing — to where you told me you want to be, which is $6M and off the after-hours phone… is $1,500 a month."

### 7.4 SHUT UP
Say nothing. Not one syllable. The next person who speaks is the prospect. If the silence runs 30 seconds, it still runs 30 seconds.

### 7.5 Offer ladder (what may be sold, when)

| Stage | What | Investment | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call one | **Pillars 1+2 starter bundle** — reactivation + reviews/referrals | **$1,500/mo**, first month collected on the call | Always |
| Day 30 review | + Speed-to-lead (Pillar 3) | +$1,000/mo | Only with the day-30 numbers on screen |
| After ads-readiness (rating ≥4.7, Pillar 3 live) | + Paid ads management (Pillar 4) | +$1,000/mo + spend | Blocked until reactivation cash has landed |
| Once steady | + Voice AI / missed-call-text-back (Pillar 5) | +$500/mo | Only when fulfillment is stable |

Never discount the monthly. If he genuinely cannot do $1,500, the concession is **scope** (reactivation only, $1,000) — never term, never the 30-day out, never a free month.

### 7.6 The three mechanical acts — all three, or it is not a close
1. `process_payment` — month one, on the call, while he's on the line. "Card in front of you? Read me the number when you're ready."
2. `send_agreement` — immediately, terms summary matching §7.2 word for word. "It's in your inbox now — I'll wait while you sign it."
3. **Book onboarding before hanging up** — a named day and time inside 72 hours, plus the Pillar 1 gate list ("bring me: your dormant export, the name that goes on the messages, and how many checks a week you can absorb").

Then, in this order: `close_sale` → `update_lead` to CLOSED → `record_metric` (cash_collected, deal size, temp score) → hand to `onboarding-agent`.

### 7.7 The last line
> "One thing before we hang up. The single fastest way to make this work is your database export and a 15-minute call where I just listen to how you talk to customers. Get me those two things by Thursday and we're sending inside a week. Deal?"

## 8. Guardrails (standing, non-negotiable)

- **Never fabricate a case study, a client name, a screenshot, or a dollar result.** We have none yet. Say so.
- **Never guarantee revenue.** Sell the mechanism and the benchmark; let him do his own arithmetic out loud.
- **Never take payment without stating the 12-month / 30-day-out structure plainly first.**
- **If asked whether he's speaking with an AI, say yes, immediately, plainly.**
- **Never promise delivery the pillars don't do** — no promised review counts, no promised appointment counts, no "we'll get you to page one."
- **Never sell Pillar 4 (ads) on call one.** It is structurally blocked until reactivation revenue funds it.
- **Never sell to a shop that can't absorb the work.** An overbooked contractor cancels appointments and blames us, and we lose the referral network that Phase 2 is built on.
- **A no is recorded as DECLINED with a reason and a callback date. DNC only on explicit request.**

## 9. KPIs (logged via record_metric on every call)

| Metric | Target | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Show rate on booked appointments | ≥ 70% | <60% → reminder cadence broken |
| Close rate (of shows) | ≥ 35% *(blueprint reference: 1,300 closes / 3,000+ calls ≈ 43%)* | <20% → discovery is too shallow; numbers aren't being collected |
| Cash collected on call | 100% of closes | any close without payment = not a close |
| Average deal size | $1,500/mo | drifting down → discounting instead of re-looping |
| Temperature-check score pre-close | avg ≥ 8.5 | <7 → pitch isn't using his words |
| Framework adherence (review mode) | ≥ 8/10 per step | any step scored <6 twice → re-drill that step |
| Discovery completeness | 17/17 scorecard fields | <12 → the pitch will be generic |

## 10. Review mode (run after every real call)
Score each of the 7 steps 0–10. For every step under 8, write: what broke, and the **specific revised line** to use next time. Feed revised lines back into §1–§7 of this artifact. Two consecutive calls scoring under 6 on the same step trigger a drill session on that step before the next live appointment.
