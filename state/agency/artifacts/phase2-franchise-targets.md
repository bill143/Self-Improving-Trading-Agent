## 0. What a "ship" is, and how these were ranked

A ship is a franchise system where **one happy franchisee introduces us to the next twenty**, because the owners already talk to each other. We are not buying a logo — we are buying access to a closed peer network.

### Filters (non-negotiable, from the brief)
| # | Filter | Test applied |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | 30+ US locations | Sourced count from franchisor site, Franchise Times Top 400 (2025), FDD-derived reviews, or brand press. Conflicts shown, not averaged away. |
| F2 | Active franchisee community | Named conference, owners' group, advisory council or peer network. Where I could not source it this pass, it is marked **UNVERIFIED** — not assumed. |
| F3 | Growth phase over last 2 years | Location count rising, active territory sales, new-brand expansion. |

### Ranking weights (why order ≠ location count)
Raw unit count is the *worst* predictor of value to us. Weighted scoring:
1. **ICP match (35%)** — does the typical franchisee run 5–50 techs with a 2,000+ record database? A 600-location system of one-truck operators is worth less than a 200-location system of $5M shops.
2. **Referral conductivity (30%)** — density and accessibility of the owner-to-owner network.
3. **Marketing autonomy (20%)** — can the franchisee actually buy from us, or does the franchisor own the marketing stack and the approval process? **This is the #1 kill-risk in franchise targeting and most agencies ignore it.**
4. **Growth signal (15%)** — new units = new owners = new buyers with a ramp-up problem.

## 4. The two structural facts that change how we sell (read this, `sales-agent`)

**(1) Franchisees are constrained buyers.** Franchise agreements typically impose brand standards on creative and may include national marketing fees. **Never pitch brand-facing advertising.** Pitch what corporate does not run:
- Pillar 1 — reactivation of *their* dormant database (their asset, their brand voice, no corporate approval needed for a service reminder).
- Pillar 3 — speed-to-lead / after-hours answer rate (an ops metric, not a marketing spend).
This positioning also sidesteps the "we already have a marketing program" objection, which will be the #1 objection in every one of these ten systems.

**(2) Buy the cluster, not the location.** Two multipliers are already proven in the data: Benjamin Franklin's ~87 owners across ~324 territories, and the One Hour / Benjamin Franklin / Mister Sparky co-ownership pattern. **Qualify every prospect with "how many locations and which brands do you run?" before quoting.** One close should land 2–4 locations.

## 5. Ship strategy — how the referral compounding actually runs
1. **Land one franchisee** in a Neighborly or Authority Brands trades system (target #1–#4).
2. **Produce a numeric result in 30 days** from the reactivation pillar — recovered jobs, dollar figure, screenshot.
3. **Ask for the network introduction, by name:** the owners' group (verified to exist at Mr. Rooter), the peer/mastermind group, the franchise advisory council.
4. **Time the push to the annual conference.** Neighborly's conference is a confirmed live event (2025: Nashville). A case study circulated the week before a conference does more than a month of cold calls.
5. **Never pitch the franchisor first.** Corporate is a gatekeeper with a competing internal marketing product. Go owner-first, let demand travel upward.
