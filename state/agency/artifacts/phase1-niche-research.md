# Phase 1 — Niche Selection Research
**Agent:** market-research-agent · **Phase:** 1 (Day 1) · **Country:** United States
**Owner context:** Bill, O'Neill Contractors — construction contracting, federal/government bidding (SAM.gov/GovTribe), trade relationships across HVAC / roofing / plumbing / solar. **STATUS: PROVISIONAL** (questionnaire not returned).

---

## 0. Method

Every candidate market was tested against the three non-negotiable filters. Any market failing one filter was cut, not softened.

| Filter | Test applied |
|---|---|
| **F1 — Physical, location-based, customer walks in (or business drives to customer's location)** | Business has a service area + physical premises; revenue is local and geo-bound. |
| **F2 — ≥$500 per transaction OR ≥$100/mo recurring per customer** | Verified against 2025 pricing data, not vibes. |
| **F3 — Proven demand: other agencies already at $100K–$500K/mo in the market** | Existence of large, named, vertical-specialist agencies. Competition = validation. |

**Cut before ranking (documented so we don't relitigate):**
- **Big-box / budget gyms** — FAIL F2. US average gym membership is ~$50/mo (multiple 2025–26 price surveys put it at $50–$65/mo; Planet Fitness anchors at $10). Only *boutique* studios clear the $100/mo bar, so the fitness market was re-cut as "boutique fitness & combat sports" and ranked on that basis.
- **Auto detailing, pest control (residential basic plans)** — FAIL F2 at typical ticket (~$50–150 recurring / <$500 job) unless ceramic-coating or commercial-only, too narrow to build an agency on.
- **General dental** — passes F1/F2/F3 but is the single most agency-saturated healthcare vertical with the longest sales cycle and heaviest compliance drag; ranked below the top 3 on speed-to-cash, not on filter failure.

## 1. RANKED RECOMMENDATION

### 🥇 #1 — RESIDENTIAL HOME SERVICES (HVAC, plumbing, electrical; roofing/solar adjacent)

**Filter evidence**
- **F1 — PASS.** Location-based service contractors with a physical shop, a truck fleet, and a defined service radius. Customer relationship is geo-locked; they cannot be served by a national competitor.
- **F2 — PASS, by a wide margin.** 2025 data: complete HVAC system replacement averages **$5,000–$12,500** installed (Armadillo, 2025), and full roof replacement averages **~$9,500–$9,600** nationally (HomeAdvisor 2025 data puts the national average at $9,607; RubyHome cites $9,526). Even a low-end service call plus a maintenance-plan enrollment clears the $500/$100-per-month test. This is a **10x–25x overshoot** of the filter, which is what makes the offers price-insensitive: a single recovered HVAC replacement pays for a year of retainer.
- **F3 — PASS.** Home services is the most heavily colonised agency vertical in the US. Named vertical specialists operating at or well above the $100K–$500K/mo band include **Scorpion** (multi-vertical, home-services-led, enterprise-scale — *high confidence it exceeds the band; exact monthly revenue not public*), **Blue Corona** (home-services-only, acquired by EverCommerce — *high confidence on the acquisition and vertical focus*), **Hook Agency** and **Rival Digital** (roofing/home-services specialists — *medium confidence on revenue scale*), plus **Josh Nelson's Seven Figure Agency**, an entire coaching community whose explicit premise is agencies hitting seven figures serving home service contractors (*high confidence the community exists and is home-services-specific; individual member revenues unverified*). Validation is unambiguous.

**Why it ranks #1 for THIS agency**
The blueprint's compounding engine is referral velocity inside a tight community. Home services gives us the three multipliers simultaneously:
1. **Highest ticket** of any market on the list → the reactivation pillar (Pillar 1) produces a demonstrable ROI inside 30 days from a database that already exists. Every HVAC company in America is sitting on 3,000–15,000 dormant service records. We can show cash before we've spent a dollar on ads.
2. **Densest franchise "ship" supply for Phase 2** — Neighborly alone umbrellas thousands of North American locations across dozens of home-service brands (Mr. Rooter, Aire Serv, Mr. Electric and others), with Authority Brands, One Hour Heating & Air Conditioning, Benjamin Franklin Plumbing and others alongside. No other market on this list offers as many 30+ location brands with live franchisee peer networks. *(Exact counts to be verified and sourced in Phase 2 — not asserted here.)*
3. **Speed-to-lead is a life-or-death metric in this trade** (Pillar 3). A no-heat call at 6pm goes to whoever answers first. That makes our AI-receptionist and 60-second-response offers a screamingly obvious sale rather than an education project.

**Unfair advantage**
> **Bill is one of them.** He runs O'Neill Contractors. He can open a call with "I run a contracting company" instead of "I run a marketing agency" — which collapses the single biggest barrier in this niche: contractors' reflexive contempt for marketers who have never held a wrench or priced a job. He already knows the language (change orders, callbacks, truck rolls, seasonal demand curves, subcontractor referral chains), he can price-check a client's job economics in real time, and — critically — **his existing subcontractor and trade relationships are a warm-intro network that no competing agency can buy.** Secondary advantage: his SAM.gov/GovTribe experience opens a commercial/government-adjacent contractor segment (facilities, mechanical, roofing subs on federal jobs) that consumer-focused agencies never touch.
>
> ⚠️ **This advantage is PROVISIONAL** — it rests on the unreturned owner questionnaire. If Bill's trade relationships turn out to be thin or non-transferable, the #1 pick still holds on F1/F2/F3 alone, but estimated time-to-first-client moves from 2–3 weeks to 4–5 weeks.

**Estimated time to first client: 2–3 weeks.** (Warm trade network + highest-ticket proof story. Contractors buy fast when the math is obvious and the seller is a peer.)

**Primary risk (stated, not hedged):** saturation. Every contractor in America gets pitched weekly. Our counter is not a better ad — it is the reactivation-first offer (we make money from a database they already own before asking for ad spend) plus Bill's insider identity. Filter 3 explicitly treats this competition as validation.

### 🥈 #2 — MED SPAS & AESTHETIC CLINICS (injectables, laser, body contouring)

**Filter evidence**
- **F1 — PASS.** Physical clinics, appointment-driven, customers walk in. Textbook fit.
- **F2 — PASS on packages/LTV, with one honest caveat.** Injectable and laser *packages* routinely run $800–$3,000+, and memberships sit well above $100/mo — clears the filter. **Caveat:** the widely-quoted ISPA figure of **~$97.50 average revenue per visit** is a spa-industry-wide average and sits *below* the per-transaction bar. The filter is satisfied by package and membership economics, not by walk-in single-visit averages. Flagging this because it changes the offer: we sell **package conversion and rebooking**, not footfall.
- **F3 — PASS.** A dedicated aesthetics-marketing agency ecosystem exists (Growth99, Moxie and similar med-spa-specific growth firms). *Medium confidence on individual agencies sitting inside the $100K–$500K/mo band — the vertical's agency layer is real and well-funded, but revenue figures are not public.*

**Why #2**
The market tailwind is the best of the three: the American Med Spa Association-sourced count of US medical spas passed **~10,500 facilities**, with forecasts near **11,500 by 2025** and roughly **1,000 new locations opening per year**; industry analysts project ~12,000 locations by 2027. Single-location med spa revenue has climbed to a reported **$1.8M–$2M annually** (JoinBlvd, 2025) from ~$1.4M in 2024. High margins, aggressive owners, marketing-native buyers who don't need convincing that ads work.

It ranks below home services on three counts: (a) **zero owner advantage** — Bill has no credibility in aesthetics and would be a generic vendor; (b) **medical/advertising compliance** overhead on claims and before/after imagery; (c) **thinner franchise ship supply** — the segment is dominated by independents and corporate-owned chains (Ideal Image, LaserAway) rather than the dense franchisee peer networks Phase 2 requires. Referral compounding would be slower.

**Unfair advantage in this market:** speed-to-lead arbitrage. Aesthetic leads are impulse-driven and decay within minutes, yet most clinics route inquiries to a front-desk staffer who is mid-treatment. A 60-second AI responder plus reactivation of lapsed Botox clients (a 12-week-cycle product with a *predictable* lapse date) is the cleanest ROI demo in any market on this list.

**Estimated time to first client: 4–6 weeks** (cold start, no warm network, longer trust-build).

### 🥉 #3 — BOUTIQUE FITNESS & COMBAT SPORTS STUDIOS (pilates, martial arts/BJJ, HIIT, recovery)

**Filter evidence**
- **F1 — PASS.** Studios; members physically attend 2–5x/week. Among the strongest walk-in dynamics of any market.
- **F2 — PASS *only* for the boutique tier.** Budget gyms fail outright (~$50/mo US average; $10/mo at the floor). Boutique pilates, martial arts and HIIT memberships sit at **$150–$250/mo**, clearing the $100/mo recurring bar. **The market must be defined as boutique-only — general "gyms" is a filter failure.**
- **F3 — PASS.** The most explicitly documented agency validation of the three: **Loud Rumor** (fitness-studio-only agency) and **Gym Launch / Alex Hormozi**, whose gym-marketing business is publicly documented at seven figures per month at its peak. *High confidence these businesses exist and are fitness-specific; treat any specific monthly figure as approximate.*

**Why #3**
Franchise ship density is excellent — Club Pilates, StretchLab, F45, 9Round, Orangetheory and similar brands run large location counts with genuinely active franchisee Facebook groups and annual conferences, which is exactly what Phase 2 wants. What drops it to third is **unit economics**: at $150–$250/mo per member, a studio owner's tolerance for a $1,500–$3,000/mo retainer is much thinner than an HVAC owner's, whose single recovered job can exceed that. Churn among studio owners is also high, and price objections arrive early and often. Excellent referral compounding, weakest cash-per-client.

**Unfair advantage in this market:** database reactivation is unusually potent here — every studio has hundreds of cancelled and expired members with contact details and a known reason for leaving. A "win back 30 lapsed members" campaign is a self-funding first month.

**Estimated time to first client: 3–5 weeks.**

## 2. DECISION

**Selected niche: Residential home services — HVAC, plumbing and electrical contractors (roofing/solar adjacent).**

Selected because it is the only market that clears all three filters *and* stacks the owner's operator credibility, the highest ticket size on the board, and the deepest supply of 30+ location franchise "ships" for Phase 2 referral compounding.

**Operating definition for downstream agents (use this exactly):**
- **Core target:** owner-operated residential HVAC / plumbing / electrical contractors, US, **5–50 technicians**, doing $1M–$15M/yr, with an existing customer database of 2,000+ records.
- **Adjacent, second-wave:** roofing and residential solar (same buyer psychology, same ticket range, Bill has trade familiarity).
- **Explicitly excluded:** single-owner-with-one-truck operators (no database to reactivate, no budget), and national/PE-rolled-up platforms (wrong buyer, procurement cycle).
- **Lead offer (feeds pillar-1 agent):** database reactivation on their dormant service history, priced against a single recovered replacement job.
